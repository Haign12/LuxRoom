# LuxRoom — Remaining Pages Design Brief

## Shared language

All remaining routes will use the LuxRoom editorial system: Newsreader for high-impact display typography, DM Sans for interfaces, mineral background, charcoal-green ink, clay highlights, fine horizontal rules and square edges. Navigation becomes a precise horizontal rail rather than a floating pill. Controls use filled charcoal or outlined text treatment; no rounded card surfaces are introduced.

## Commerce routes

| Route | Layout principle | Primary purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Collection | Editorial index with a text masthead and breathable product grid | Browse objects by room, material and mood |
| Cart | Two-column ledger | Review objects and move to checkout without friction |
| Checkout | Sequential form paired with a stable order summary | Enter delivery and payment details with confidence |
| Success | Quiet confirmation composition | Confirm order and offer a clear return to collection |
| Account | Personal collection ledger | Surface profile, saved preferences and order history |
| Sign in | Split editorial introduction + focused form | Make account access feel considered, not transactional |

## Brand and support routes

| Route | Layout principle | Primary purpose |
|---|---|---|
| About | Manifesto and material-driven chapter layout | Explain LuxRoom's point of view |
| Contact | Introductory statement, contact details and consultation form | Invite thoughtful conversations |

## Interaction and accessibility

The existing local cart model remains in place. Quantity inputs, removals, checkout confirmation, search overlay and navigation are preserved. Forms use readable labels, focus states and responsive single-column fallbacks. Essential UI has direct text labels, while non-essential reveal motion respects reduced-motion preferences.

## Deliberate distinctions

The collection page uses a typographic index rather than the homepage's image-first stories. Cart and checkout use a ledger rhythm rather than promotional panels. About and Contact use oversized display headlines and editorial columns rather than product grids. This creates cohesion without repeating one page pattern across every route.
